# Product-image rights and external review research

**Checked:** 14 October 2026 (Europe/Berlin). **Scope:** commercial editorial pages on milchpumpe-vergleich.de with Amazon affiliate links, plus Pinterest posts promoting those articles. Research only: no product images acquired, no accounts accessed, nothing published. This is an evidence assessment, **not legal advice or a guarantee of permission**.

## Decision summary

- **No blanket permission was established to download public product-page images for any of the six models.** A public image, download button, press release, Amazon affiliate tag or credit line is not, by itself, evidence covering the intended use.
- **Momcozy offers the clearest explicit conditional route:** approved affiliates may use the creatives provided through their affiliate account on websites and social media. This does **not** establish permission to scrape public product photos or repurpose the creatives for Amazon-linked comparisons rather than Momcozy's own affiliate campaign; obtain confirmation and the applicable network agreement first.[3]
- **Philips explicitly requires written authorization for its brand assets**, including photos. Its media library invitation to enrich stories does not resolve commercial affiliate/Pinterest scope for SCF547/11.[4][5]
- **Elvie's current website terms reserve image rights and prohibit unlicensed reuse.** Its press-enquiry page supplies a permission-request contact, not a reusable public image licence.[6][7]
- **Medela and Haakaa: no exact-model commercial image licence verified.** Official media/affiliate channels exist, but availability of assets or an application form is not a grant to this publisher.[8][29]
- **External hands-on reporting was found**, especially F.A.Z. Kaufkompass, Mumsnet, MadeForMums and Mother&Baby. These are editorial/user-experience sources, often affiliate-funded, **not independent laboratory or clinical tests**. Philips SCF547/11 and Haakaa Generation 3 have more limited exact-model review evidence; see the variant cautions below.

## 1. Image-reuse evidence and clearance gates

### Amazon: authorized delivery is not a downloadable photo library

The public German programme policies make the publisher responsible for respecting third-party rights and forbid misleading product claims.[1] The public IP licence limits content use to participation in the programme, display on the publisher's Site, and directing users to the relevant Amazon pages.[2]

**Verbatim evidence, IP licence §2(viii):**

> “Sie werden keine Inhalte von Produktwerbung, die aus einem Bild bestehen, speichern oder zwischenspeichern, aber Sie können einen Link zu Inhalten von Produktwerbung, die aus einem Bild bestehen, bis zu 24 Stunden speichern.”[2]

Practical consequence: do not download Amazon product photos into `public/`, screenshot listings, cache/proxy image bytes, or turn them into Pinterest collages under an assumed Amazon licence. The 24-hour provision concerns the **image link**, not permission to store the image. Refresh and display requirements, content notices, link destinations and the rest of the agreement also apply.[2]

A Pinterest pin using Amazon-provided content but linking to an editorial article needs separate clearance: the licence says not to direct traffic to non-Amazon websites in connection with that content. Do not assume that ordinary affiliate-link permission covers reuploading Amazon photos to Pinterest.[2]

**Account-dependent route:** provision an eligible Amazon account and currently authorized product-data API/tool access, confirm the current service/version and Site registration requirements, then implement the permitted remote-image/link lifecycle. The retrieved public licence uses the name “PA-API”; this research did not authenticate into PartnerNet or establish which interface this account can use. The supplied project context states that credentials/API access are not configured. No account eligibility or end-to-end attribution has been verified.

## 2. Credible external reviews — exact models and limitations

**Classification:** “editorial hands-on” means a named publication reports actual parent use; “affiliate review” means commercial links/disclosure accompany that work. Neither label establishes laboratory independence, standardized output measurements, medical suitability or copyright permission for the publication's photos. Use attributed paraphrases, not copied images, test seals or invented firsthand results.

### Momcozy Mobile Flow M9

- **F.A.Z. Kaufkompass, Leonora Bajraktari, _Die beste Milchpumpe_.** Visible article update **20 July 2026**; update log says **14 July 2026**; HTML `dateModified` says **23 September 2026**. These are different fields, not a new observed hands-on test date. It reports testing the **M9 double set** and recommends it for app control. Positives: easy Bluetooth/app operation, separate sealable collectors and flexible settings. Reservations: weight in the bra and a bulky transport box. **Affiliate-funded editorial hands-on**, not a lab.[18]
- **MadeForMums, Libby Galvin, 22 May 2026.** Exact M9 review, double-pump context. Useful practical caution: controls/app are convenient, but it is not a forget-it-is-there pump and can leak when bending forward. This article explicitly discloses affiliate commission.[10]
- **Mumsnet, Jenny Wonnacott; updated 19 August 2025** (live page/metadata). Named parent-tester Alex used the **double M9 regularly for three months**. Reported easy first setup and useful travel/storage design, but volume-recording app glitches and less effective expression for this tester than the Elvie/Medela pumps she previously used. Affiliate revenue disclosed; no controlled comparison or transferable milk-yield result.[11]

**Methodology quote, F.A.Z.:**

> “Weil sie von zu vielen individuellen, variierenden Voraussetzungen abhängt, beurteilen wir dabei nicht die abgepumpte Milchmenge.”[18]

The same methodology reports everyday testing by a mother of twins, at least four practical tests per pump, and assessment of assembly, handling, portability, settings, cleaning and suction behaviour.[18] This is a useful limitation to preserve prominently.

### Momcozy S12 Pro — original

- **F.A.Z. Kaufkompass, same dated comparison.** A separate **“Momcozy S12 Pro”** section expressly reports testing the double set. Good build/handling and multiple modes, but weak suction in the lower settings for the tester, noticeable noise, alignment sensitivity and possible spilling when removing it. The page separately tests **S12 Pro Quick** as an upgrade: its quietness/stronger-suction assessment must **not** be transferred to the original.[18]
- **Mother&Baby, Emily Gilbert; updated 8 September 2025.** Article expressly names **S12 Pro** and parent-tester Sophie. She praised cushioned flanges, battery life and mobility; negatives include a visible/bulky motor, fiddly cleaning/assembly and unclear instructions requiring online help. **Editorial parent-use/shop-linked review**, not a laboratory result; an exact test duration or sample-procurement arrangement was not verified.[12]

**Conflict worth retaining:** Mother&Baby calls it quieter than other pumps, while F.A.Z. criticizes noise. These are relative impressions from different experiences, not comparable measured decibel tests. Do not reconcile them into a made-up sound rating.[12][18]

### Medela Freestyle Hands-free — original

- **MadeForMums, Jessica Lever; published 1 February 2024.** Exact Hands-free double model, not Mini. Named tester Jess reports lightweight wearable cups, straightforward cleaning/app use and convenient movement, but expense, audible operation and difficulty judging expressed volume. Incorrect membrane placement impaired her pumping. **Editorial parent-use review**, not clinical efficacy evidence.[13]
- **Mumsnet, Jenny Wonnacott; updated 19 August 2025** (live page/metadata). Parent-tester Charlotte used the pump **once daily for four weeks**. She found it comfortable/easy to assemble; reservations include noise in a shared office and sensitive motor controls in a pocket. Incomplete cup engagement caused a leak despite an apparent click. Affiliate-funded editorial review.[14]

**Evidence quote:**

> “Our parent-tester Charlotte used the Medela Freestyle Hands-free Breast Pump once a day over four weeks.”[14]

### Philips Avent SCF547/11 Hands-free double

- **No verified exact-SKU established-publication hands-on test was found in this research.** Other Philips Avent models (SCF395/11, SCF398/31, SCF430/01) are not evidence for SCF547/11.
- **Testbericht.de, exact SCF547/11 listing:** the page explicitly says **“Noch keine Testberichte”**. It contains attributed user reports, not a publication's own product test. “Mimi”, March 2026, reports satisfactory suction but dislikes that battery status must be called up by button; “Alina”, January 2026, reports daily use and an assembly-related leakage incident. These are **unverified user reports hosted by a comparison portal**; purchase verification, incentivization and original provenance were not established.[20]
- **Galaxus magazine, Stephan Lamprecht, _The hands-free double breast pump from Avent delivers_.** A named friend, Lena, used the pump for a few weeks after a product presentation. She liked comfort/assembly/cleaning, disliked the dangling motor and fiddly emptying. **Retailer-hosted parent trial**, not an independent lab. The extracted article does **not** establish SKU SCF547/11 or a publication date: keep it a **model-family candidate only**, not exact-model proof. Cleaning comments are user descriptions, not instructions to override the current manufacturer manual.[19]

### Elvie Pump Single — not Stride

- **GoodtoKnow, _Elvie Single Electric Breast Pump review_; published 10 February 2022** (HTML metadata). It expressly tests the **Single** version, using named parent Lisa Murphy-O'Reilly. Practical findings: mobility/discretion and normal-bra use were helpful; bra tightness, fully dry components and correct seals affected suction; bending/lying down sometimes caused leaks. **Editorial parent trial with shopping links**, not an independent lab or proof of performance for every user.[15]
- **F.A.Z. Kaufkompass, same comparison.** The “Elvie Pump” section describes **two pumps**, so it supports the original Pump model family, not a separately tested Single package. It praises app handling and quiet use, but reports needing to recharge after roughly an hour of its use. Do not substitute this observed duration for a current manufacturer specification or extrapolate it to every battery/session.[18]

### Haakaa Generation 3

- **MadeForMums, Hazelann Williams; published 2 February 2023.** Title/URL explicitly say “Generation 3”, and the named tester describes attachment difficulty, discomfort and limited expressed volume. **However, the body describes a single-piece silicone pump requiring no assembly**, whereas the manufacturer's Gen 3 page specifies a bottle base, attachment ring and flange. **Generation identification is therefore questionable.** Do not carry its rating or alleged exact-model test results onto a Gen 3 product page without clarification from the publication.[16][25]
- **The Analytical Mommy, _Haakaa Silicone Breast Pumps review_.** HTML: published **22 July 2018**, modified **8 May 2021**. Explicit affiliate disclosure. Author says she owns/uses the third-generation version and distinguishes its multiple pieces, heavier size and conversion to a feeding bottle from the older one-piece pump. **Attributed affiliate-blog experience**, not lab evidence; old prices, comparative suction language and quantified milk claims are not validated current measurements. Useful for a clearly attributed modular-design/cleaning trade-off, not a “testsieger” claim.[26]
- **F.A.Z. tests Haakaa Generation 2**, not Gen 3; its findings must not be transferred.[18]

**Primary model check:**

> “This pump is made up of 1 x Silicone Bottle base, 1 x Attachment Ring and 1 x Silicone Pump Flange attachment.”[25]

### Stiftung Warentest and weaker comparison material

- No named Stiftung-Warentest pump test/grade for these exact models was located. The retrieved primary test.de article **_Probleme beim Stillen: Bloß nicht zu früh aufgeben_**, **19 September 2014**, is advice on breastfeeding problems, **not a product comparison or model recommendation**. This is not proof that no test exists anywhere; it is a documented research gap. Do not invent a Warentest score/seal.[27]
- **Tell Me Baby, updated March 2025**, compares Elvie Pump, Freestyle Hands-free and original S12 Pro among other models. The extraction contains specifications and comparative calculations, but did not establish a standardized measurement protocol, sample procurement, calibration or clinical test. Treat it as supplementary comparative material, not lab corroboration; do not reproduce its decibel ranking as verified measurement.[17]

## 3. Editorial and acquisition handoff

1. Keep factual specification tables grounded in the **exact current manufacturer manual/SKU**. Keep editorial impressions separately attributed to the named external reviewer and date.
2. Suitable copy: “Mumsnet-Testerin Charlotte empfand die Freestyle Hands-free als komfortabel, kritisierte aber die Lautstärke.” Do not write “wir haben getestet”, fabricate a tester, invent milk-output results or adopt another publisher's score as this site's own.
3. Request an asset-specific licence naming: the domain; Amazon affiliate monetization/destination; neutral comparison use; organic Pinterest article promotions; any paid Pinterest advertising separately; Germany/worldwide internet distribution; duration; crop/resize/text-overlay rights; photographer/model releases; required credit; and revocation procedure.
4. Acquire only after clearance through the licensor's approved channel. Preserve exact model, asset ID, source URL, grant/email/agreement, acquisition date, authorized platforms and credit. **No assets are currently marked cleared or acquired by this research.**
5. Until then use original commissioned/self-shot exact-product photos with documented rights, or original non-photographic editorial graphics that do not falsely depict a product or imply firsthand testing. Review/test photographs found elsewhere remain separately copyrighted.

## 4. Retrieval limits

Search used the configured service's Exa fallback because local SearXNG was unreachable. One browser attempt timed out; several extractors omitted sections, so text-only live HTML was used to verify Philips terms and review dates. No image/CDN downloads were made. Amazon's direct HTML request returned an incomplete/interstitial response; conclusions above rely on its successfully extracted public licence/policy text, not that response. A Medela `/en/terms-of-use` candidate returned 404 and was not treated as legal authority.[22] A Momcozy terms extraction contained unrelated “Creative Commons” wording, so it was rejected as image-licence evidence rather than interpreted as a CC grant.[23] Public media-kit searches are not exhaustive; unresolved rights require written clearance, not an assumption that no licence can exist.
